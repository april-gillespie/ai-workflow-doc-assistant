Field Note: Informal Customer Discovery at Museum

Today I tested the small-business workflow/service idea in a real-world setting by initiating conversations with museum staff. My original intent was to ask research questions and gather a data point, but the interaction naturally shifted based on what the other person was excited about.

Key observations:

* People are willing to talk when approached with genuine curiosity.
* A rigid pitch would likely be less effective than asking about their current projects, frustrations, and goals.
* The strongest entry point may be encouragement and practical helpfulness, not “selling AI.”
* Relationship-building appears to be a viable discovery channel.
* Publicly recognizing helpful employees can build goodwill and trust quickly.

Business relevance:
This supports the hypothesis that a low-pressure, service-based offer could work best when framed around helping small organizations document, organize, and improve existing workflows rather than selling them a vague “AI solution.”

Potential offer language:
“I help small businesses turn messy processes, scattered notes, and repeated explanations into simple documents, checklists, templates, or AI-assisted workflows.”
